# Open Lab Code of Conduct

Open Lab exists to make useful things better through open practitioner collaboration.

Different industries, disciplines, organizations, and experiences will produce different answers.

That's useful.

Disagreement is expected.

## The Basics

**Challenge the work, not the person.**

Question assumptions. Point out flaws. Offer alternatives. Explain why you disagree.

Don't attack the person who contributed the idea.

**Share what you know. Be honest about what you don't.**

Experience matters, but nobody has seen every environment or solved every version of a problem.

**There are no sacred tools.**

Everything here can be questioned, changed, improved, forked, or replaced.

That includes the tools, the documentation, and the way Open Lab itself operates.

**Don't confuse publication with validation.**

A tool appearing in Open Lab does not make it a standard, best practice, regulatory expectation, validated solution, or universally appropriate approach.

**Protect what isn't yours to share.**

Do not contribute confidential, proprietary, customer-specific, employee-specific, controlled, or otherwise restricted information.

Bring the lesson.

Don't bring somebody else's intellectual property.

**AI is a tool, not accountability.**

AI-assisted contributions are welcome.

Contributors remain responsible for understanding, reviewing, and standing behind what they submit.

**Don't turn the Lab into a sales funnel.**

Relevant attribution is appropriate.

Using the community primarily to harvest contacts, advertise services, or push unrelated products isn't.

## One Rule Above the Rest

Be willing to explain your thinking.

And be willing to change it.

We're here to make the work better.
